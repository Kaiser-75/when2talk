When2Talk overview. Event-triggered (ET) communication speaks immediately at every predefined ride event. Context-sensitive (CS) communication selects Immediate, Delayed, or Silent delivery based on Event Priority and Passenger Activity.

Key Findings

Across 41 participants, context-sensitive communication mainly changed when and whether the vehicle spoke, while the canonical event message stayed fixed whenever both policies communicated.

Appropriateness
CS 5.09 · ET 4.50

Communication appropriateness was higher under CS (p = .019).

Interruption
CS 2.14 · ET 3.71

CS substantially reduced perceived interruption (p < .001; lower is better).

Trust
CS 4.96 · ET 4.95

Overall trust did not differ between the two policies (p = .959).

Usefulness
ET 4.51 · CS 3.71

ET was rated more useful overall, driven by Silent contexts where CS withheld explicit information.

Most importantly: delayed communication improved appropriateness and reduced interruption while preserving usefulness, whereas silence reduced interruption but could remove information or confirmation that some passengers valued.

Abstract

Proactive in-cabin agents can help passengers understand automated-vehicle (AV) behavior, but communicating every ride event may introduce unnecessary interruptions. We investigated how communication should adapt to event priority and passenger activity. In a mixed-methods within-subject study, 41 participants rode as passenger in a VR simulated fully-automated vehicle. We compared an event-triggered (ET) policy that communicated immediately at every event with a context-sensitive (CS) policy that selected Immediate, Delayed, or Silent communications. CS increased communication appropriateness and substantially reduced perceived interruption. Perceived trust did not differ between policies, although baselines dispositional trust differentiated communication preferences. Findings highlight event consequence, passenger activity, continuing information value, and confirmation need as key considerations for selective in-cabin communication.

Study Design

A 2 (Communication Policy) × 2 (Event Priority) × 3 (Passenger Activity) within-subject design. Each participant experienced both ET and CS in all six Priority × Activity contexts.

Road Monitoring, Intermittent Phone Use, and Sustained Phone Use were crossed with High- and Low-Priority ride events. ET was Immediate in every context; CS used the prespecified Immediate, Delayed, or Silent action.

CARLA/VR Passenger Study

Participants experienced scripted CARLA 0.9.15 ride events from the front passenger seat through a Varjo XR-4 HMD. A synchronized smartphone application controlled passenger activities and questionnaires, and the headset recorded eye-tracking data at 200 Hz.

Overall Results

The main policy comparison shows the central trade-off: CS reduced interruption and improved appropriateness, trust remained unchanged, and ET retained an overall usefulness advantage because it always provided explicit information.
